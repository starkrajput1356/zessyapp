About the Blood Pressure Tracker

Log and track your blood pressure readings over time, see them classified against AHA/ESC categories (normal, elevated, stage 1, stage 2, crisis), view trends and averages on a chart, and export a clean PDF or CSV log for your doctor.

Readings are stored privately on your device; add notes such as time of day, medication and activity to spot patterns like morning hypertension or white-coat effect.

How to use the blood pressure tracker

  1. 1Enter systolic, diastolic and pulse; add date, time and notes.
  2. 2See the category instantly and how it compares with your average.
  3. 3Review the trend chart and weekly/monthly averages.
  4. 4Export a PDF/CSV log or print it for your next appointment.

Blood Pressure Tracker — frequently asked questions

What is a normal blood pressure reading?

Below 120/80 mmHg is normal. 120–129 systolic with diastolic below 80 is elevated; 130–139/80–89 is stage 1 hypertension; 140+/90+ stage 2; above 180/120 is a crisis requiring urgent care.

How often should I measure blood pressure?

For home monitoring, twice in the morning and twice in the evening for 7 days, then average — the log does this automatically.

Why are my readings different each time?

Blood pressure varies with posture, caffeine, stress, talking and cuff position. Sit quietly for 5 minutes, feet flat, arm at heart level, and take two readings a minute apart.

What does pulse pressure mean?

Systolic minus diastolic. Above 60 mmHg in older adults can indicate stiff arteries; the tracker shows it for each reading.

Can I share my log with my doctor?

Yes — export a PDF or CSV with all readings, averages and notes.
